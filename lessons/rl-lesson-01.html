MDPs & the Bellman Equation

The whole field is one idea wearing different costumes: value is a fixed point you solve by stepping one move forward and looking back. This lesson builds that idea from the agent–environment loop up.

01 The formal object

It’s a Markov Decision Process

RL formalizes that loop as an MDP: states S, actions A, dynamics p(s′,r | s,a), and a discount γ ∈ [0,1). The one assumption doing all the work is the Markov property: the next state and reward depend only on the current (s,a), not the full history. The state is a sufficient statistic for the future — that’s what makes the math tractable.

Where it breaks for you When the true problem isn’t Markov — partial observability, e.g. a screen-observation Dota/League agent — you either stuff history into the state or move to a POMDP. Worth flagging now; it returns later.
02 The objective

Maximize return, not reward

The agent doesn’t chase the next reward — it chases the discounted sum of all future reward, the return:

returnGt = r₊₁ + γ·r₊₂ + γ²·r₊₃ + …

The discount γ keeps the sum finite and says “sooner is better.” But its real meaning is an effective horizon ≈ 1/(1−γ) steps.

A real knob, not a default For a 45-minute game, γ is the dial deciding how far credit flows back from a win. Copying 0.99 without thinking about your episode length is a classic silent mistake.

04 The one idea

The Bellman equation

Value is recursive. A state’s value equals the expected immediate reward plus the discounted value of wherever you land next:

bellman expectationvπ(s) = Σa π(a|s) · Σs′,r p(s′,r|s,a) · [ r + γ·vπ(s′) ]

Read it as a self-consistency condition: vπ is the unique function that agrees with itself one step forward. This little diagram is the equation — average over policy branches (blue), then over environment outcomes, collecting reward and discounted next-value (amber):

Every algorithm is a way to solve this fixed point

That last row is the whole game, and it’s Lesson 2.

05 Going optimal

Swap the average for a max

The Bellman optimality equation replaces “average over what the policy does” with “take the best action”:

bellman optimalityv*(s) = maxa Σs′,r p(s′,r|s,a) · [ r + γ·v*(s′) ]
06 Check yourself

One exercise

In a deterministic environment with γ = 0, what does q*(s,a) reduce to — and what does greedy-on-q* give you? Try it in your head before opening.

SOLVE Reveal the answer ▸

With γ = 0 the future term vanishes, so q*(s,a) = r(s,a) — just the immediate reward. Greedy-on-q* becomes “pick the highest-immediate-reward action”: a pure myopic / bandit policy.

That’s the cleanest possible statement of what γ buys you: it’s the dial between total myopia (γ=0) and full long-horizon planning (γ→1). Everything interesting in RL lives in between.

Takeaway

Every method in RL solves the same Bellman fixed point. They differ only in two choices: do they know the dynamics, and do they bootstrap?
