Hardware · the physical machine DATA CENTERS & SCALING

When the Building Is the Computer

Scale up far enough and the unit of compute stops being the chip, or even the rack — it becomes the data center, drawing the power of a small city. At that scale a new master constraint takes over from the memory wall: not FLOPs, not bandwidth, but electricity and heat. The chip now drives the building, not the other way around.

01 The new binding constraint

It’s power, not chips

The defining inversion of this era: in 2024 the bottleneck was GPU supply; now it’s electricity. The chips are available — the power to run them is not. Data-center vacancy is near zero not because servers fill the space but because electrical capacity is fully committed; in the largest markets, new grid connections can take years.

02 Getting the heat out

Air cooling is over

Every watt in becomes a watt of heat out, and density broke the old method. Air cooling tops out around 20 kW per rack; modern GPU racks start near 40 kW and climb past 120. So liquid cooling is now mandatory, not optional: direct-to-chip cold plates pipe coolant straight onto the GPUs (liquid moves heat ~3,500× better than air), and immersion submerges whole boards in dielectric fluid. NVIDIA’s newest rack is fully liquid-cooled, fanless, and cableless — air cooling it isn’t inefficient, it’s physically impossible.

Cooling is a first-class cost, not plumbing Cooling can be 30–40% of a data center’s electricity, and liquid infrastructure adds serious capex per megawatt. This is why the field’s efficiency metric shifted: not PUE, not FLOPS-per-dollar, but tokens per watt. The blunt framing operators use: revenue = tokens-per-watt × available-gigawatts. When power is the ceiling, every architectural trick you’ve learned (FlashAttention, MoE, KV compression, quantization) is ultimately a play to push more tokens through the same watts.

03 Scaling’s hidden taxes

Reliability and the supply chain

Two problems appear only at the top of the scale. First, reliability: a training job spanning tens of thousands of GPUs for weeks will certainly hit hardware failures — a dead GPU, a failed NVLink switch tray, a flaky optical link. Because the job is tightly coupled, one failure can stall everyone, so frequent checkpointing and fault tolerance aren’t nice-to-haves; they’re what makes a long run finish at all.

Second, the bottleneck cascades down the supply chain. Behind the power wall sits a packaging wall — TSMC’s CoWoS (which stacks HBM next to the compute die) is booked out — and behind that an HBM wall, with SK Hynix/Samsung/Micron sold out generations ahead. You can have money and grid power and still wait on advanced packaging.

Train burst, infer forever The load shape matters. Training is bursty — a run starts, consumes hugely, ends. Inference is continuous: every deployed model draws power 24/7 and now dominates total AI energy. That’s why the 2026 buildout is tilting toward inference-optimized, sometimes distributed/edge capacity — and why “tokens per watt” beats raw FLOPs as the number that matters once you’re serving real traffic.

04 The whole stack, one view

Co-design, top to bottom

Step back and the entire hardware branch is one idea seen at different altitudes. Arithmetic intensity on the chip; bandwidth cliffs between chips; power and heat across the building. At every level the move is the same — keep the data close to the compute, and spend bytes and watts as if they’re the scarce resource, because they are. And the causality runs both ways: hardware shapes models (FP8/FP4 training, MoE for the all-to-all fabric, models sized to HBM) just as models shape hardware (silicon built matmul-native because the transformer won).

This is the floor under your build Your rack-scale evaluation — MI300X/MI325X vs B200, MoE serving, disaggregated prefill/decode — is a decision made on exactly these three axes at once: roofline fit, interconnect topology, and power/cooling envelope. There is no “best”; there is only the configuration that maximizes useful tokens per watt for your workload at your scale. That’s the lens the whole branch was building toward.
