Pouring a Big Model Into a Small One

Distillation is how a giant, expensive model teaches a small, cheap one to behave like it. The trick isn’t copying weights — it’s copying judgement: the student learns from the teacher’s full probability distribution, not just its final answer. It’s the bridge between your RL branch and your hardware branch — the step that turns a frontier reasoner into something you can actually serve.

01 The core idea

Soft targets and “dark knowledge”

The original insight (Hinton, 2015): a one-hot label throws away almost everything. If the answer is “7,” the label [0,0,…,1,…,0] tells the model nothing about the fact that a 7 looks a little like a 1 and nothing like an 8. But the teacher’s soft output — 7: 0.9, 1: 0.07, 8: 0.001… — encodes exactly those similarities. Hinton called this the dark knowledge: the structure hiding in the small probabilities.

The R1 recipe — reasoning, distilled The most consequential example: DeepSeek-R1. Massive RL (GRPO) on a 671B MoE teacher produced structured, verifiable reasoning traces. Then ~800K curated traces (≈600K reasoning + 200K general) were used to plain-SFT small students — Qwen 1.5B→32B, Llama 8B/70B. No logit matching, no student rollouts; just SFT on the teacher’s chains of thought. The headline: this transmits multi-step reasoning into compact models where direct RL barely works — a 7B model can’t easily discover reasoning on its own, but it can inherit it. One study hit near-o1-preview math with only ~17K traces.

03 The frontier move

On-policy distillation: RL’s structure, a teacher’s richness

Offline SFT on teacher traces has a quiet flaw you met in the RL branch: exposure bias. The student only ever sees the teacher’s perfect trajectories, never its own mistakes — so at inference, one wrong token drifts it into states it was never trained on, and errors compound. On-policy distillation fixes this exactly the way RL does: the student generates its own rollouts, and the teacher grades them — but densely, scoring every token rather than handing back one final reward.

The tidy mental model On-policy distillation ≈ GRPO with the verifier swapped for a teacher. Same on-policy loop you studied — sample from the student, score, update toward higher score — but the reward is the teacher’s dense token-level judgement instead of a sparse 0/1 from a test or answer-checker. That density is why it’s often far more sample-efficient than RLVR for transferring a skill the teacher already has.

04 The fine print

Where it bites — and why you’d still do it

Distillation is not magic compression. Three honest limits:

Format over substance. A student SFT’d on chains of thought often learns to imitate the shape of reasoning — the long, hedging CoT style — without the underlying competence. It can reach the right answer through steps that are individually wrong. The trace looks like thinking; it isn’t always thinking.

A small-model ceiling. Capacity still rules. Distilling R1 into 1.5B craters hard reasoning (MATH dropping ~25 points) even as latency improves ~16×; the 32B/70B students keep most of the teacher’s ability, the ≤7B ones keep a fraction. You’re trading capability for speed on a real curve.

Safety can erode. Aggressively distilling raw reasoning traces can wash out a model’s prior alignment — refusal rates drop, jailbreaks land more often — unless you deliberately distill safety back in.

Why it’s still everywhere — your two branches, joined Because it’s the deployment step of the frontier loop: spend enormous RL compute training one giant teacher, then distill it into a family of small models cheap enough to actually serve. That’s your hardware branch (smaller student = more tokens per watt, fits the memory budget) meeting your RL branch (the teacher’s expensively-learned reasoning, transferred for pennies). Frontier labs ship the big model’s intelligence inside small models’ economics — and that round-trip is distillation.

Q1 What is the "dark knowledge" that makes distillation work?

The shape of the distribution. A one-hot label only says which token is correct. The teacher's soft output also says a "7" was a bit like a "1" and nothing like an "8" — encoding similarity structure in the small probabilities. The student minimizes KL to that full distribution (often with a temperature), learning far more per example than a hard label provides.

Q2 How were the DeepSeek-R1 distilled models (Qwen/Llama) actually trained?

Sequence-level (offline) distillation. The 671B R1 teacher (trained with GRPO) generated ~800K curated traces; small students were simply supervised-fine-tuned on that static text. The striking result: this transmits multi-step reasoning into models too small to discover it via direct RL — they can't find it, but they can inherit it.

Q3 What problem does on-policy distillation solve that offline SFT-on-traces does not?

Train-test mismatch. Offline SFT shows the student only the teacher's flawless paths, so at inference one slip drifts it into unseen states and errors compound. On-policy distillation has the student generate its own sequences and the teacher grade them, so it learns to recover from its own mistakes — exactly the on-policy benefit from the RL branch.

Q4 What's the clean mental model relating on-policy distillation to GRPO?

Same loop, denser reward. Both sample from the student, score, and update toward higher score. RLVR's reward is a sparse 0/1 from a verifier; on-policy distillation's "reward" is the teacher's dense token-level signal. That density makes it far more sample-efficient for transferring a skill the teacher already has — though it needs a stronger teacher to exist.

Q5 Which is a real limitation of distilling reasoning into small models?

Not free compression. A student can imitate the look of a chain of thought — reaching right answers via individually-wrong steps — without genuine competence. Capacity still binds: distilling R1 into 1.5B craters hard reasoning (~25-pt MATH drop) even at ~16× speedup, and aggressive trace distillation can wash out prior safety alignment unless re-instilled.
Takeaway

Distillation transfers a teacher's judgement — its whole distribution, not just its answer — into a smaller model. It runs from plain SFT on teacher traces (the R1 recipe that puts reasoning in models too small to learn it directly) to on-policy distillation, which is essentially GRPO with a dense teacher reward instead of a sparse verifier. It's the deployment step of the frontier loop: where your RL branch (the teacher's costly intelligence) meets your hardware branch (the student's cheap economics).
